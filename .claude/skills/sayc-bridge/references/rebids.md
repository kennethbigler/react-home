# Rebids — Opener and Responder (SAYC)

The second round of bidding is where partnerships describe strength precisely.
The key idea: each player's first two bids together pin down their **point range**
and **shape**.

## 1. Opener's rebid framework — three strength bands
After 1-of-a-suit and a response, opener's rebid shows one of:
- **Minimum (12–15):** rebid suit at the cheapest level, raise responder to the
  2 level, or rebid 1NT (12–14 in SAYC after a 1-level response).
- **Invitational / medium (16–18):** jump rebid own suit, jump raise responder,
  or rebid 2NT (18–19 balanced).
- **Game-forcing / maximum (19–21):** jump to game, jump shift, or reverse with
  extras; 2NT/3NT rebids show the big balanced hands.

## 2. Opener's rebid by hand type

| Opener's rebid | Shows |
|----------------|-------|
| Rebid own suit (1♥–1♠–2♥) | 6+ card suit, minimum (12–15) |
| Jump rebid own suit (1♥–1♠–3♥) | 6+ card suit, invitational (16–18) |
| Rebid 1NT (1♣–1♥–1NT) | **12–14** balanced, denies 4 of responder's major |
| Jump to 2NT (1♣–1♥–2NT) | 18–19 balanced |
| Rebid 3NT | ~19–20 balanced with stoppers, or to play |
| Raise responder (1♣–1♥–2♥) | 4-card support, minimum (12–15) |
| Jump-raise responder (1♣–1♥–3♥) | 4-card support, invitational (16–18) |
| Raise to game (1♣–1♥–4♥) | 4-card support, ~19–21, no slam slam-driving needed |
| New lower suit (1♣–1♥–1♠ / 2♣) | Natural, shows a second suit, not forcing if cheap |
| Reverse (see §3) | A second suit higher-ranking, **17+**, forcing one round |

> Note the **1NT rebid is 12–14** (because 15–17 balanced would have opened 1NT).
> This is a frequent source of confusion: opener's 1NT *rebid* ≠ 1NT *opening*.

## 3. Reverses
- A **reverse** is when opener bids a new, higher-ranking suit at the 2 level that
  forces responder to give preference at the 3 level — e.g. 1♦–1♠–**2♥** (hearts
  rank above the original diamonds and force responder past 2♦ to take a
  preference). 1♣–1♠–2♥ likewise.
- Shows **17+ HCP** and **longer first suit than second** (e.g. 5+ diamonds, 4
  hearts). It is **forcing for one round**.
- Why the strength requirement: the reverse drives the auction to the 3-level, so
  opener must have the values to be safe there opposite a minimum response.
- Responder with a minimum gives simple preference; with more, jumps or bids on.

## 4. Jump shift by opener
- 1♦–1♠–**3♣** (a jump in a new suit) = ~19+, game-forcing, big two-suiter.
  Stronger than a reverse; sets up a force.

## 5. Responder's rebids
Responder's second bid clarifies the 6–12 vs 13+ question opener is asking.
- **Preference / simple rebid (e.g. back to opener's first suit):** minimum
  (6–9), no game interest.
- **Invitational jump (e.g. 1♥–1♠–2♣–... 3♣, or jump in own suit):** ~10–12.
- **New suit at the 2-level (after a 1-level start), non-jump:** still forcing if
  responder is unpassed and it's a genuine new suit; check level carefully.
- **Raising opener's second suit, or rebidding own 6-carder:** shows that shape;
  level shows strength.
- **2NT / 3NT by responder:** natural, shows the corresponding balanced range
  (2NT ≈ 11–12 invite, 3NT ≈ 13–15 to play).
- **Fourth-suit forcing** and **new minor forcing** (below) are the artificial
  game-try tools.

## 6. Fourth-suit forcing (FSF)
- When responder bids the **one unbid suit** after three suits have been
  mentioned (e.g. 1♣–1♥–1♠–**2♦**), it is **artificial and forcing** (game-forcing
  by default in SAYC). It does **not** promise that suit.
- It says: "I have game-going values but no clear bid — describe further
  (do you have a stopper for NT? extra length? support?)."
- Opener then bids NT with a stopper in the fourth suit, raises responder, rebids
  a suit, etc. FSF buys time and room to find the right strain/level.

## 7. New minor forcing (NMF)
- After opener rebids **1NT** (1♣–1♥–1NT), responder's bid of the **other minor**
  (here 2♦, the "new minor") is artificial and forcing, asking opener to show
  3-card support for responder's major or a 4-card other major.
- Used when responder has invitational+ values and a 5-card major and wants to
  check for a 5-3 major fit before committing to 3NT.
- Opener's replies: bid responder's major with 3-card support, show a 4-card
  other major, else rebid NT. (Some pairs play "checkback Stayman"/2-way NMF as a
  variant — flag it; SAYC default is standard NMF.)

## 8. Forcing vs non-forcing — quick summary
**Forcing:**
- New suit by an unpassed responder (1-level or 2-level).
- Reverse by opener (one round).
- Jump shift (opener or responder).
- Fourth-suit forcing; new minor forcing.
- 2♣ opening and its game-forcing auctions.
- Cue-bid of opponents' suit.

**Non-forcing (limited) bids:**
- Raises of partner's suit (single, limit) — invitational at most.
- 1NT, 2NT, 3NT rebids — show their range, may be passed.
- Rebid of one's own suit at the minimum level.
- Preferences back to partner's first suit.
