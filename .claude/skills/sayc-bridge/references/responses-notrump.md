# Responding to Notrump Openings (SAYC)

Covers responses to **1NT (15–17)** primarily; the same machinery applies to
**2NT (20–21)** and after a 2♣–2♦–2NT rebid (22–24), just shifted in level. The
1NT opener is captain's *servant*: responder places the contract.

## 1. Quick map of responses to 1NT (15–17)

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Pass | 0–7, balanced, no 5-card major |
| 2♣ | **Stayman** — asks for a 4-card major (8+ for game tries; or "garbage") |
| 2♦ | **Jacoby transfer to ♥** (5+ hearts) |
| 2♥ | **Jacoby transfer to ♠** (5+ spades) |
| 2♠ | **Transfer to a minor / range probe** (variant; see §6) |
| 2NT | Invitational, 8–9 HCP, balanced, no 5-card major |
| 3♣ / 3♦ | Natural, forcing, 6+ suit, slam-ish (or use 4-suit transfers) |
| 3♥ / 3♠ | Natural, forcing, 5+ suit, game-forcing slam try (variant: invitational) |
| 3NT | To play, 10–15 HCP, no 5-card major, no slam interest |
| 4♦ / 4♥ | **Texas transfers** to ♥ / ♠ (game-level, see §5) |
| 4♣ | **Gerber** (ace ask), see §8 |
| 4NT | Quantitative slam invite, ~16–17, balanced (see §9) |
| 5NT | Quantitative grand-slam invite, forces to 6NT minimum |
| 6NT | To play, ~18–19 combined-slam values |

## 2. Raises and the game/slam math
- 1NT opener has 15–17. Add your HCP:
  - 8–9 → invite (2NT). Opener passes with 15, bids 3NT with 17 (judgment on 16).
  - 10–15 → 3NT (with no 5-card major).
  - 16–17 → 4NT quantitative; 18 → 6NT; ~20+ → explore grand.
- With a **5-card major**, transfer first, then raise NT or rebid the major to
  show the rest of the hand (lets opener choose between the major and notrump).

## 3. Stayman (2♣)
- Asks "do you have a 4-card major?" Requires either game-going values **or** a
  specific weak shape (garbage Stayman).
- **Opener's replies:**
  - 2♦ = no 4-card major.
  - 2♥ = 4 hearts (may also have 4 spades).
  - 2♠ = 4 spades (denies 4 hearts).
- **Responder's follow-ups:**
  - Raise opener's major to 3 = invitational with 4-card support.
  - Raise to 4 = to play, 4-card support, game values.
  - 2NT = invitational, no fit found (8–9).
  - 3NT = game values, no major fit (had exactly 4 of the unbid major).
  - New suit = forcing, shows shape / slam interest.
- **Garbage / drop-dead Stayman:** weak hand (0–7) short in clubs but with both
  majors or 4-4-4-1 / 4-4-5-0 shapes — bid 2♣ intending to pass any response
  (you'll land in a 4-3 or better major fit rather than 1NT). Requires being
  short in diamonds too so you can pass 2♦.
- **Do not** use Stayman with a 4-3-3-3 game-going hand and no real major
  interest — just bid 3NT.

## 4. Jacoby transfers (2♦ → ♥, 2♥ → ♠)
- Shows **5+** cards in the target major; opener is **required** to complete the
  transfer (bid the major) so the strong hand declares and stays concealed.
- After the transfer completes, responder describes:
  - Pass = weak, just wanted to play 2 of the major (5+ suit, < 8).
  - 2NT = invitational, exactly 5 in the major (opener picks: pass/3M/3NT/4M).
  - 3 of the major = invitational, 6+ suit.
  - 3NT = game values, exactly 5 in the major (opener picks 3NT or 4M).
  - 4 of the major = to play, 6+ suit, no slam interest.
  - New suit = game-forcing, second suit, slam interest.
- **Super-accept:** with 4-card support and a maximum (17), opener may jump
  (e.g. 1NT–2♦–3♥) to show the great fit and encourage.

## 5. Texas transfers (4♦ → ♥, 4♥ → ♠)
- A transfer at the **game level**: 6+ card major, game values, no slam ambition
  beyond game. Opener completes to 4 of the major.
- Lets the strong 1NT hand declare 4-major. Use Texas (vs a Jacoby transfer then
  jump) to keep the auction clean for the "I just want game" hand. With slam
  interest, transfer at the 2 level (Jacoby) first to leave room.

## 6. Four-suit / minor-suit transfers (the 2♠ response, variant)
Common SAYC add-on; flag as a variant since pure SAYC just plays 2♠ as natural.
- **2♠ = transfer to clubs** (opener bids 3♣; responder passes or continues), and
  a 2NT-ish range probe in some structures.
- In a full four-suit transfer scheme: 2♦→♥, 2♥→♠, 2♠→♣ (then pass/correct to ♦),
  and 2NT can be used as a relay. Use only if the convention card specifies it.

## 7. Smolen
- Use after Stayman when opener denies a major (1NT–2♣–2♦) and responder has
  **5-4 in the majors with game-forcing values** (5 in one major, 4 in the other).
- Responder **jumps in the 4-card major** at the 3-level: 1NT–2♣–2♦–**3♥** shows
  4 hearts + 5 spades; **3♠** shows 4 spades + 5 hearts (you jump in the shorter
  major to point opener at your longer one). This lets the 1NT opener, who may
  hold 3 cards in responder's 5-card
  major, declare game in the 5-3 fit. Opener bids the known 5-card major (game)
  with 3-card support, else 3NT.

## 8. Gerber (4♣ over notrump)
- A **direct jump to 4♣ over a natural 1NT or 2NT** is Gerber, asking for aces.
  (Over a suit opening, 4♣ is natural/splinter, NOT Gerber — Gerber is only over
  notrump.)
- Responses (ace-count):
  - 4♦ = 0 or 4 aces
  - 4♥ = 1 ace
  - 4♠ = 2 aces
  - 4NT = 3 aces
- A follow-up **5♣** then asks for kings by the same step scheme. Use Gerber when
  notrump is the likely strain and you need aces before bidding 6NT.

## 9. Quantitative 4NT
- A direct raise **1NT–4NT** is **not** Blackwood — it is quantitative, inviting
  6NT: "bid 6NT if you're maximum (17), pass if minimum (15)." Opener judges with
  16. Similarly **1NT–5NT** invites a grand and forces at least 6NT.

## 10. Responses to 2NT and to 2♣–2♦–2NT
- All the above (Stayman, transfers, Texas, Gerber, quantitative) apply, shifted
  up one level: over **2NT**, 3♣ = Stayman, 3♦→♥, 3♥→♠ transfers, 4♣ = Gerber,
  4NT = quantitative.
- After **2♣–2♦–2NT** (22–24 balanced), treat exactly like a 2NT opening:
  Stayman and transfers are on.
