# Responding to a 1-of-a-Suit Opening (SAYC)

Partner opened 1♣, 1♦, 1♥, or 1♠. As an **unpassed responder**, a new suit is
forcing; you almost always bid with 6+ HCP. With 0–5 you pass.

## 1. Response priorities
With a normal hand, in order:
1. **Raise partner's major immediately with 3+ support** (showing a fit is the
   single most valuable message). Strength sets the level.
2. **Bid a new suit at the 1 level** (cheap, forcing, keeps options open) —
   especially a 4-card major up the line.
3. Bid a new suit at the 2 level (needs more strength — see 2/1).
4. Bid notrump (denies a fit and a biddable new suit at the cheap level).

Bid 4-card suits **"up the line"** at the 1 level (1♦–1♥ rather than skipping to
spades) when you have both; bid the longer suit first when 5+.

## 2. Raising partner's major (1♥ / 1♠) — needs 3+ trumps
Count **support points** (HCP + shortness) once the fit is found:

| Response | Meaning | Support pts |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| Single raise (1♥–2♥) | Simple raise, non-forcing | 6–10, 3+ trumps |
| Limit raise (1♥–3♥) | Invitational jump raise | 10–12, 4+ trumps |
| Jacoby 2NT (1♥–2NT) | Game-forcing raise, 4+ trumps, 13+ | see §8 |
| Splinter (e.g. 1♥–3♠/4♣/4♦) | Game-forcing raise + shortness | 11–14, 4+ trumps |
| 1♥–4♥ (direct game) | Preemptive: 5+ trumps, weak (≈ < 10 HCP), shapely | shape-based |

- A **limit raise** is invitational, NOT forcing. Opener passes with a minimum.
- The direct **jump to game** (1♠–4♠) is *weak and shapely* (lots of trumps,
  little defense) — it is NOT a strong hand. Strong hands go via Jacoby 2NT or a
  splinter.

## 3. Raising partner's minor (1♣ / 1♦) — needs 4+ (often 5+)
- Minors raise less often because majors and NT pay better. With a 4-card major,
  bid the major first.
- Single raise 1♣–2♣ / 1♦–2♦: 6–9(10), no 4-card major, denies a better bid.
- Inverted minors (variant, common): a **single** raise (1♦–2♦) = 10+ forcing,
  and a **jump** raise (1♦–3♦) = weak/preemptive. Note this is the inverse of
  natural; flag it as a variant — SAYC's pure default is natural (single = weak,
  jump = invitational), but inverted minors are widely added.

## 4. New suit at the 1 level
- 6+ HCP, 4+ cards, forcing one round. Bid 4-card suits up the line.
- 1♣–1♦–1♥–1♠ ordering: respond in the cheapest 4-card suit; with two 4-card
  suits bid the cheaper first; with 5-5 bid the higher first.
- Always prefer showing a **4-card major** over raising a minor or bidding 1NT.

## 5. New suit at the 2 level (two-over-one)
- A non-jump 2-level new suit (e.g. 1♠–2♣, 1♥–2♦) shows **10+ HCP** (some play a
  full game force in a "2/1 GF" system; SAYC's default is 10+ and forcing one
  round, *not* an absolute game force). The suit is 4+ (usually 5+ if a minor
  over a major).
- You may not bid a new suit at the 2 level on a weak hand — use the forcing 1NT
  instead (§7).

## 6. Notrump responses
| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 1NT (over 1 of a suit) | 6–10 HCP, no fit, no biddable new suit — see §7 re: forcing |
| 2NT | Natural 13–15 (if not playing Jacoby) / **Jacoby 2NT** when playing it over a major |
| 3NT | 16–17 balanced (variant: some play 13–15 here and 2NT as the limit raise) |

Over a **major**, 2NT is reserved for **Jacoby 2NT** (game-forcing raise), so a
natural balanced game-going hand without a fit must find another route (jump in
NT or bid a suit then NT). Over a **minor**, 2NT is natural and invitational/
game-forcing per partnership style.

## 7. The forcing (or semi-forcing) 1NT response — over a MAJOR
- After 1♥ or 1♠, a 1NT response is **forcing for one round** (SAYC default;
  "semi-forcing" is the common variant where opener may pass with a dead 5-3-3-2
  minimum).
- It shows **6–12 HCP** with no fit and is a catch-all (it can contain an
  invitational hand, a weak hand, or a hand with a long minor it will bid next).
- Opener must rebid (if forcing): show a second suit, rebid the major with 6,
  or bid a minor on 3 if necessary. Responder then clarifies.
- Over a **minor** opening, 1NT is natural and **non-forcing** (6–10).

## 8. Jacoby 2NT (1♥/1♠ – 2NT)
- A game-forcing raise: **4+ trump support, 13+ points, balanced-ish** (no
  singleton — with shortness use a splinter instead).
- Asks opener to describe slam interest:
  - **New suit at the 3 level** = a singleton/void in that suit (shortness).
  - **3 of the agreed major** = a strong, balanced minimum interested in slam.
  - **4 of the agreed major** = a minimum with no slam interest, signoff.
  - **New suit at the 4 level** = a strong 5-card side suit (second suit), slam
    interest.
  - **3NT** = ~15–17 balanced, no shortness, mild slam interest (variant
    orderings exist; the key split is "shortness vs minimum vs extras").

## 9. Splinter bids
- A **double jump** in a new suit agreeing partner's major shows a game-forcing
  raise (4+ trumps, ~11–14) with a **singleton or void** in the bid suit.
  - Examples: 1♥–3♠, 1♥–4♣, 1♥–4♦; 1♠–4♣, 1♠–4♦, 1♠–4♥.
- Tells opener exactly where your shortness is so they can judge slam: facing
  wasted honors opposite your shortness, sign off; facing useful controls, drive.

## 10. Bergen raises (variant — flag it)
Played by many over a major; not pure SAYC but a common add-on:
- 1♥/1♠ – **3♣** = constructive raise, 4+ trumps, ~7–10.
- 1♥/1♠ – **3♦** = limit raise, 4+ trumps, ~10–12.
- 1♥/1♠ – **3 of the major** = preemptive, 4+ trumps, weak.
- Requires 4-card support; with only 3 use a normal single/limit raise.

## 11. Jump shift by responder (strong)
- A jump in a new suit (1♦–2♠, 1♥–3♣) = **17+ HCP** (or a great suit), strongly
  forcing, slam-interested. Sets up a force and lets the partnership cue-bid.
- (Weak jump shifts are a different agreement; SAYC default is **strong**.)

## 12. Passed-hand responses (Drury)
- A passed hand can't have a strong raise, so jumps are off.
- **Drury** (common add-on): after partner opens 1♥/1♠ in 3rd/4th seat and you
  passed originally, **2♣** is artificial showing a limit raise (3+ trumps,
  ~10–12). Opener rebids 2 of the major to show a light 3rd-seat opener (signoff)
  or otherwise shows a real opening and accepts. This stops you from getting too
  high opposite a light third-seat opening.
