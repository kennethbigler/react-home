# Competitive Bidding (SAYC)

When opponents enter the auction, the priorities change: you compete for the
contract, fight for partscores by the Law of Total Tricks, and use doubles to
describe rather than to penalize (mostly).

## 1. Simple overcalls (e.g. (1♦) 1♠)
- A 1-level overcall: ~**8–16 HCP**, a **good 5+ card suit** (quality matters
  more than points — you want a suit worth bidding and leading).
- A 2-level overcall: sounder, ~**10–17**, good 5+ (usually 6) card suit, because
  you're at a higher level vulnerable to penalty.
- Overcalls are **not forcing**. Partner raises with a fit, bids a new suit
  (forcing only by a cue-bid; a new suit by overcaller's partner is constructive
  but typically non-forcing), or cue-bids to show a real raise (see §8).

## 2. Jump overcalls (weak)
- A jump overcall ((1♦) 2♠) is **weak** in SAYC: like a weak two-bid, a good
  6-card suit and ~6–11 HCP. Preemptive, robbing space.
- Double jump ((1♦) 3♠) = weaker still / preemptive 7-card suit.

## 3. 1NT overcall and balancing 1NT
- **Direct 1NT overcall** ((1♥) 1NT) = **15–18** balanced with a **stopper** in
  opener's suit. Systems on (Stayman/transfers) in responses.
- **Balancing 1NT** (after (1♥) Pass Pass) = lighter, **~11–14** (sometimes
  12–14), because you're protecting partner's possible values.

## 4. Takeout doubles
- A double of a suit opening at a low level, by a player who has not yet bid the
  suit, is **takeout**: "I have opening values and support for the unbid suits,
  partner pick a suit."
- Classic shape: **opening values (12+), shortness in opener's suit, 3+ cards in
  each unbid suit** (especially the unbid majors).
- Strong takeout: **17+** — double first, then bid your own suit to show a hand
  too strong to overcall directly.
- A takeout double of 1♦ ideally has both majors; of 1♥ has spades + minors; etc.

## 5. Responding to a takeout double
Partner doubled (e.g. (1♥) Dbl (Pass) to you):
- You are (usually) **forced to bid** unless RHO bids — even with 0 points, pick
  your best suit at the cheapest level.
- **0–8:** bid your longest/best suit at the minimum level (a "non-jump" = could
  be nothing).
- **9–11:** **jump** in your suit (invitational), or jump-cue.
- **12+:** **cue-bid opener's suit** (game-forcing, asks doubler to describe), or
  jump to game.
- A **1NT response** to a takeout double shows ~6–10 with a stopper in opener's
  suit; **2NT** ~11–12 with a stopper.
- **Passing for penalty** (converting takeout to penalty) requires a strong
  trump holding behind opener — rare but powerful.

## 6. Negative doubles
- A double by **responder** after partner opens and RHO overcalls (e.g. 1♣ (1♠)
  **Dbl**) is **negative (takeout-flavored)**: it shows values and the **unbid
  suit(s)**, especially the **unbid major(s)**.
- Classic example: 1♣ (1♠) Dbl shows **4+ hearts** and enough to compete
  (~6–9 at the 1-level; more to drive higher). 1♦ (1♥) Dbl shows **both majors
  (4-4)** typically.
- Negative doubles are "on" through a partnership-agreed level (commonly through
  **2♠** or **3♠**; SAYC default through 2♠). Opener responds as if to a normal
  suit/level message: bids the implied suit, jumps with extras, etc.
- The benefit: lets responder show a major after an overcall without needing to
  bid it at an awkward level.

## 7. Responsive and support doubles (variants — flag them)
- **Responsive double:** after (1♥) Dbl (2♥) **Dbl**, the second double by the
  takeout-doubler's partner is takeout, showing the unbid suits when opponents
  have raised. Common add-on.
- **Support double:** after 1♥ (Pass) 1♠ (2♦), opener's **double** shows
  **exactly 3-card support** for responder's suit (a redouble/double distinction);
  a raise shows 4. Common expert add-on; flag as a variant.

## 8. Cue-bid raises (limit-raise-or-better)
- When partner overcalls and you have a genuine **limit raise or better** in
  their suit, **cue-bid opener's suit** ((1♦) 1♠ (Pass) **2♦**) rather than just
  raising. A plain raise of an overcall is competitive/weak-to-moderate; the cue
  shows the good raise so you don't miss game.

## 9. Michaels cue bids (two-suited overcall)
- A **direct cue-bid of opener's suit** shows a **5-5 two-suiter**:
  - Cue of a **minor** (1♣/1♦) → **both majors** (5-5 in hearts and spades).
  - Cue of a **major** (1♥/1♠) → the **other major + an unspecified minor**
    (5-5). Partner bids 2NT to ask which minor.
- Two strength zones: weak (preempt-ish) or strong (good 5-5); typically
  *not* a middling hand (those just overcall one suit).

## 10. Unusual 2NT (two-suited overcall)
- A **jump to 2NT directly over an opening** ((1♠) **2NT**) is the **Unusual
  Notrump**, showing the **two lowest unbid suits** — typically **both minors**
  (5-5+). Over a minor opening it shows the other minor + a major per agreement.
- Together with Michaels, this gives a complete two-suited-overcall toolkit:
  Michaels = the "touching/major-oriented" pairs, Unusual 2NT = the two lowest.

## 11. Balancing (protective bidding)
- In the **pass-out seat** (e.g. (1♥) Pass Pass to you), bid more aggressively —
  "borrow a king" from partner who is marked with values by their pass.
- Balancing actions: a light double (~8+ with takeout shape), a light overcall, a
  **balancing 1NT (11–14)**, or a balancing 2NT showing the minors.
- Don't sell out to a 1-of-a-major opening cheaply when you have shortness in
  their suit — reopen so partner's trumps/values aren't wasted.

## 12. The Law of Total Tricks (LOTT)
- **The Law:** the total number of tricks available to both sides ≈ the total
  number of **trumps** both sides hold in their respective best fits.
- Practical use: bid to the level equal to the number of trumps your side holds —
  with a **9-card fit, compete to the 3-level**; an **8-card fit, the 2-level**;
  a **10-card fit, the 4-level** — when deciding whether to push on or sell out in
  a competitive partscore battle.
- It guides **competing, not slamming**, and assumes reasonable distributions;
  adjust down for flat hands and "minus" honors in the opponents' suits, up for
  pure shape and double fits.

## 13. Penalty doubles
- In SAYC most low-level doubles are takeout/negative. A double is **penalty**
  when:
  - It can't logically be takeout (e.g., partner already showed a suit; opponents
    are clearly overboard; a double of a notrump contract).
  - It is above the negative-double range (e.g., a double after the negative-
    double level has passed).
- Doubling a **freely bid game/slam** or a **voluntarily bid 1NT/2NT** is
  penalty. Doubling a **preempt** is takeout (optional/"do something
  intelligent"). When in doubt at low levels, assume **takeout** in SAYC.
