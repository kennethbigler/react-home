# Defense to Opponents' Conventions (SAYC)

How to bid when opponents open or use a convention against you. These are the
common SAYC-compatible defensive methods.

## Table of contents
1. Defending against a 1NT opening
2. Defending against weak two-bids and preempts
3. Defending against a strong 2♣
4. Lebensohl (after interference over your 1NT, and over weak twos)
5. Defending against transfers and Stayman

---

## 1. Defending against a 1NT opening
You need a way to compete over a strong notrump. SAYC players use one of:

**Cappelletti (a.k.a. Hamilton)** — the common default:
- **Double** = penalty (a hand ≈ equal/stronger than the 1NT, expecting to set
  them).
- **2♣** = a one-suited hand (a single long suit, partner bids 2♦ to ask, you
  pass or correct to your suit).
- **2♦** = both majors (5-4 or 5-5).
- **2♥** = hearts + a minor.
- **2♠** = spades + a minor.
- **2NT** = both minors.

**DONT (Disturb Opponents' NoTrump)** — common alternative:
- **Double** = a one-suited hand (then bid the suit).
- **2♣** = clubs + a higher suit.
- **2♦** = diamonds + a higher suit.
- **2♥** = hearts + spades.
- **2♠** = spades (one-suiter).
- The point of DONT: get into the auction cheaply with any two-suiter; double is
  *not* penalty.

> SAYC's convention card lets you choose; **Cappelletti** is the most common
> SAYC pairing. Note which the partnership plays since the meaning of "double"
> differs (penalty in Cappelletti, one-suiter in DONT).

## 2. Defending against weak two-bids and preempts
- **Double = takeout** (opening values, support for unbid suits, shortness in
  their suit). Partner responds as to any takeout double, jumping with extras.
- **2NT over a weak two = 15–18 balanced** with a stopper (like a 1NT overcall),
  natural; systems-on in responses by agreement.
- **A new suit = natural overcall**, sound values (you're at the 2–3 level).
- **Cue-bid of their suit** = a strong takeout / Michaels-style two-suiter by
  agreement, or a strong hand asking partner to describe.
- Against 3-level preempts: double is takeout; **3NT** is to play (you have a
  stopper and a source of tricks); a new suit is a sound overcall.
- **Lebensohl** can apply over a weak two after partner's takeout double (see §4)
  to distinguish weak from invitational advances.

## 3. Defending against a strong, artificial 2♣
- After (2♣) — before responder has shown anything — a **double** of 2♣ is
  typically **lead-directing for clubs**, and an overcall is natural and sound.
- More useful: defend **after the 2♦ waiting response** — then doubles of their
  steps are lead-directing/takeout by agreement. Most pairs simply overcall
  naturally and use double as lead-directing in the artificial suit bid.

## 4. Lebensohl
A two-context convention; flag as a common add-on (not pure SAYC core):

**(a) After partner's takeout double of a weak two:**
- Advancer's **2NT is artificial**, forcing partner to bid 3♣; advancer then
  passes or corrects — this shows a **weak** hand wanting to play a partscore.
- Bidding the suit **directly** at the 3-level (without going through 2NT) shows
  an **invitational+** hand. This "slow shows weak, fast shows strong" split
  (sometimes "slow = strong" depending on partnership agreement — clarify which)
  lets the partnership distinguish a sign-off from a game try after the preempt
  ate their bidding room.

**(b) After your 1NT is overcalled (e.g. 1NT (2♥)):**
- **2NT** = relay to 3♣ (puppet), then pass/correct = a weak signoff in a lower
  suit; a direct 3-level suit = game-forcing.
- Doubles, cue-bids of their suit (Stayman-ish), and 3NT placements distinguish
  "stopper shown vs not" via the slow/fast 3NT route.

> Lebensohl's whole purpose is to let you sign off in a partscore **or** force to
> game after an opponent's bid steals your natural invitational bid. Note it as a
> variant and confirm the slow/fast convention orientation with the card.

## 5. Defending against transfers and Stayman
- When opponents open 1NT and use Stayman/transfers, a **double of an artificial
  bid (the 2♣ Stayman or a 2♦ transfer) is lead-directing** (shows that suit),
  not penalty.
- Bidding their transfer-target suit, or doubling, lets your side compete or get
  the right lead. Keep it simple: double = "lead this suit," bids = natural.
