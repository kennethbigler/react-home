# Hand Evaluation (SAYC)

All SAYC bidding rests on evaluating your hand correctly. Evaluation is not
static — it changes as the auction reveals fit and as you learn partner's shape.

## 1. High Card Points (HCP)
- Ace = 4, King = 3, Queen = 2, Jack = 1.
- Total in the deck = 40. Average hand = 10 HCP.
- Rough partnership thresholds (combined HCP):
  - **Part-score** territory: < 25.
  - **Game** (3NT, 4♥/4♠, 5♣/5♦): ~25–26 (majors/NT), ~28–29 (minors, because
    11 tricks is harder than 10/9).
  - **Small slam** (6-level): ~33.
  - **Grand slam** (7-level): ~37.

## 2. Distribution points
Two schemes; use the one that matches the contract type.

**Length points (use when evaluating as opener / for notrump-ish hands):**
- Add 1 point for each card beyond 4 in a suit (5-card suit = +1, 6-card = +2…).

**Shortness/support points (use when you have found a trump FIT and will be the
supporting/dummy hand):**
- Void = 5, singleton = 3, doubleton = 1 — but only count shortness once you
  know partner has length there (i.e., once a fit is agreed and you're raising).

> Do **not** double-count: count length points when describing your hand, switch
> to shortness points once a trump fit is found and you are the dummy.

## 3. When to count length vs shortness
- **Opener / no fit yet:** count HCP + length points.
- **Raising partner's suit (you'll be dummy):** count HCP + shortness points.
- **Notrump contracts:** count HCP only (+1 for a strong 5-card suit, but
  shortness is a *liability* in NT, not an asset).

## 4. The Rule of 20 / 22 / 15 (deciding to open)
- **Rule of 20:** Open at the 1-level if **HCP + (length of two longest suits) ≥
  20**. E.g. 11 HCP with 5-5 shape (11+10=21) → open. This is the SAYC light-open
  guideline for 11–12 HCP hands.
- **Rule of 22 (stricter):** require HCP + two longest suits + quick tricks ≥ 22.
  A more conservative gate some players use.
- **Rule of 15 (4th seat only):** in 4th seat (after three passes), open only if
  **HCP + spades ≥ 15**, because holding spades wins competitive auctions and you
  don't want to reopen a dead hand into a minus score.

## 5. Quick tricks
Defensive/honor strength, used for 2♣ decisions and competitive judgment:
- AK (same suit) = 2
- AQ = 1.5
- A = 1
- KQ = 1
- Kx = 0.5
A strong 2♣ opener typically has ~4+ quick tricks.

## 6. Losing Trick Count (LTC)
A trick-based evaluation for **suit contracts once a fit is found**. Count
losers in each suit (max 3 per suit, only over the first three cards):
- A suit's losers = (missing among A, K, Q in the first 3 cards).
- AKQ = 0 losers; AKx = 1; Axx = 2; xxx = 3; Kx = 1; singleton K = 1; singleton
  other = 1; doubleton Ax = 1; void = 0.
- **Formula:** Expected tradeable tricks ≈ 24 − (your losers + partner's losers),
  for the partnership. Practical use: a typical opening hand ≈ 7 losers, a strong
  response ≈ 9 losers.
  - If your losers + partner's estimated losers point to 10 tricks → bid game.
  - Old rule of thumb: `tricks = 18 − (combined losers)` for the two hands; many
    use `24 − combined losers` counting both hands fully. Use it directionally,
    not as gospel.

## 7. Re-evaluation after a fit
This is where good bidding is won. When a trump fit (8+ cards) is found:
- Promote short suits to shortness points (void 5 / singleton 3 / doubleton 1).
- A side 4-card suit and ruffing values gain value.
- Honors in partner's known long suits gain value; honors in opponents' suits or
  in your own short side suits lose value.
- A hand worth 12 as opener can be worth 15+ "in support" once a big fit and
  shortness combine.

## 8. Adjustments and danger signs
**Upgrade** (count a little more):
- Lots of aces and kings (controls), tens and nines (intermediates), suits with
  honors concentrated together (KQJ better than scattered K, Q, J).
- A good long suit (source of tricks).

**Downgrade** (count a little less):
- Honors in short suits, especially unsupported (singleton K, Qx, Jx) — they may
  be worthless.
- "Quacks" (lots of queens and jacks, few aces).
- 4-3-3-3 flat shape — the most pessimistic distribution; downgrade slightly.

## 9. Notrump vs suit evaluation
- For **notrump**, value HCP and intermediates; stoppers in all four suits
  matter; shortness is a minus.
- For **suit** play, value controls (aces/kings), trump length, and shortness for
  ruffing.
- A balanced 15–17 with a good 5-card major can be opened 1NT in SAYC, but bid
  the major when in doubt about reaching the right strain.
