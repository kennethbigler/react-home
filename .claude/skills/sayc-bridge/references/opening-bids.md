## 2. One of a suit
- Open ~13+ HCP automatically; open 11–12 with shape per the **Rule of 20**.
- Maximum for a 1-level suit opening is ~21 HCP; with 22+ balanced open 2♣ (or
  2NT/3NT rebid range).
- Minors can be short: 1♣ may be only 3 cards (the "convenient minor"); 1♦ is
  usually 4+ but can be 3 in the specific 4-4-3-2 shape with 3 diamonds.

## 3. Which suit to open (decision order)
1. **5-card major?** Open it. With two 5-card majors (5-5), open **1♠** (higher
   first, to rebid the lower cheaply).
2. **No 5-card major:** open the **longer minor**.
3. **Minors equal length:**
   - 4-4 minors → open **1♦**.
   - 3-3 minors (the 4-4-3-2 / 4-3-3-3 type with no major) → open **1♣**.
4. **Two 5-card suits, not both majors:** open the **higher-ranking** suit
   first, then rebid (or rebid) the lower — this conserves bidding space.
5. With 6-4, open the 6-card suit first.

## 4. 1NT opening (15–17 balanced)
- Balanced = no void, no singleton, at most one doubleton: shapes 4-3-3-3,
  4-4-3-2, 5-3-3-2.
- A 5-card major in a 5-3-3-2 15–17 hand may be opened 1NT (SAYC allows it);
  judgment call — open the major if the hand plays better in a suit.
- Responses live in `references/responses-notrump.md` (Stayman, transfers, etc.).

## 5. 2NT opening (20–21 balanced)
- 20–21 HCP, balanced. Forcing to game-ish (responder rarely passes).
- Responses: Stayman (3♣) and transfers (3♦→♥, 3♥→♠) apply, just one level up.
  See `responses-notrump.md`.
- With 22–24 balanced: open 2♣ then rebid 2NT. With 25–27: 2♣ then 3NT.

## 6. 3NT opening (gambling)
- SAYC default: a solid, running **7-card minor** (AKQxxxx or better) with little
  outside strength — partner expects to run the minor and pass or pull to the
  minor with no outside stoppers.

## 7. Strong, artificial 2♣
- The only strong forcing opening. **22+ balanced** OR a hand with **~9+ playing
  tricks** (e.g. a long, strong suit good for game on its own).
- It says nothing about clubs — it is artificial and **game-forcing** (except the
  one exception below).
- **Responses to 2♣:**
  - **2♦ = waiting** (default, says nothing; most common). Some play 2♦ as the
    negative — but SAYC default is 2♦ waiting.
  - **2♥/2♠/3♣/3♦ = positive**, a genuine suit with ~8+ HCP and a 5-card suit
    with two of the top three honors (some play these as natural positives).
  - **2NT = positive balanced**, ~8+ HCP (variant; many just bid 2♦ then NT).
- **Opener's rebid after 2♦ waiting:**
  - 2NT = 22–24 balanced (Stayman/transfers now apply).
  - A suit = natural, game forcing; responder may then show a second negative.
  - 3NT = 25–27 balanced.
- **The single non-forcing sequence:** 2♣–2♦–2NT may be passed by a totally
  broke responder (rare in practice). Otherwise 2♣ forces to game.

## 8. Weak two-bids (2♦ / 2♥ / 2♠)
- **Default range 5–11 HCP** (many play 6–10); a **good 6-card suit**, typically
  two of the top three honors, no 4-card major on the side, not a strong hand.
- Not vulnerable, can be lighter/more aggressive; vulnerable, sounder.
- **Responses:**
  - Raise of the suit = preemptive (further preempt / Law of Total Tricks), not
    invitational.
  - **2NT = the one forcing inquiry** (asks opener to describe). SAYC default is
    **"Ogust"** or "feature": opener shows a feature (an outside A/K) with a max,
    or rebids the suit with a minimum. (Ogust variant: 3♣ = bad hand/bad suit,
    3♦ = bad hand/good suit, 3♥ = good hand/bad suit, 3♠ = good hand/good suit,
    3NT = AKQ of the suit.)
  - New suit by responder = natural and forcing (rare).
- 2♦ is also a weak two in SAYC (not the strong/Flannery treatment).

## 9. Preemptive 3- and 4-level openings
- **3-level (3♣/3♦/3♥/3♠):** good 7-card suit, < opening values (~6–10), used to
  rob bidding space. Vulnerability matters: "sound at red, wild at white."
- Guideline: the **Rule of 2-3-4** — overbid by no more than the number of tricks
  that keeps you within 2 (vulnerable vs not / unfavorable), 3, or 4 of your
  contract depending on vulnerability.
- **4-level major openings:** 7–8 card suit, shape-based, intended to play or
  push opponents to the 5-level. 4♣/4♦ as natural preempts are rare.

## 10. Third- and fourth-seat openings
- **Third seat (after two passes):** may open lighter and on a good suit you want
  led; a third-seat 1-of-a-major can be a 4-card suit "lead-directing" light
  opener. Subsequent jump-shift responses are off (partner is a passed hand).
- **Fourth seat (after three passes):** use the **Rule of 15** — open only if HCP
  + spade length ≥ 15. Don't reopen a flat 11-count that will go minus; do open
  hands with spades since you can win the spade battle.
