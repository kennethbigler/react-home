# Play and Declarer Technique (post-auction)

Bidding sets the contract; play wins or loses it. This is a primer on the core
declarer-play and defensive-play techniques a SAYC player needs. It is not part
of the bidding system, but it is essential context for a bridge app.

## 1. Planning the play
Before playing to trick one, declarer should:
- **Count sure winners** (notrump) or **count losers** (suit contracts).
- Compare to the contract: how many more tricks do I need / how many losers must
  I shed?
- Identify the **source of extra tricks** (long suit, finesse, ruff, promotion).
- Note **entries** to each hand and the **danger hand** (the defender who must
  not gain the lead).
- Only then play to trick one. A small card held up at trick one is often right.

## 2. Counting
- **Winners (NT):** count immediate top tricks, then plan how to develop more.
- **Losers (suits):** count losers in each suit (as in LTC), then plan to ruff,
  discard, or finesse them away.
- **Count the hand:** track the distribution and high cards as the play unfolds;
  good defenders and declarers count out all four hands.

## 3. Notrump declarer play
- Usual plan: develop your **longest combined suit** for length tricks.
- **Hold up** a stopper (duck early rounds) to exhaust one defender's cards in
  the danger suit, cutting communication — classic on a long-suit lead.
- Knock out entries to the danger hand before establishing the dangerous suit.
- **Rule of 7** (when to hold up an ace vs a long suit on lead): hold up the
  number of times = 7 − (total cards you and dummy hold in the suit).

## 4. Suit declarer play (trumps)
- Decide **whether and when to draw trumps**. Draw them when you have no need for
  dummy's trumps to ruff; delay when you need to ruff losers in dummy first.
- "Don't draw trumps if doing so strands your ruffing tricks." Count how many
  trumps you can spare.
- **Crossruff:** cash side winners first, then ruff back and forth — but cash
  side aces before you start ruffing or they may get ruffed away.
- **Dummy reversal:** ruff in the *long* trump hand and draw trumps with the
  short hand when it gains a trick.

## 5. Finesses
- A finesse is an attempt to win a trick with a card while a higher card is
  outstanding, by leading toward the honor.
- **Lead toward** the honor you hope to score (e.g., lead low toward AQ hoping
  the king is onside / to your left).
- **Two-way / double finesse**, **ruffing finesse**, and **backward finesse**
  are situational refinements.
- Prefer a finesse only after counting whether a non-finesse line (drop, length,
  endplay) is better. "Eight ever, nine never" — with 8 cards missing the queen,
  finesse; with 9, play for the drop (a guideline, not a law).

## 6. Establishing and cashing
- **Establish** long suits by conceding early so the small cards become winners.
- Time it so you still have an **entry** to cash them after they're good.
- Cash winners in the right order to preserve entries and avoid blockages
  (unblock high cards from the short hand first).

## 7. Entry management
- Plan entries before touching a suit. Count entries to the hand that holds the
  established length.
- Preserve a high card in the short hand as an entry; overtake to reach a long
  suit when needed.

## 8. Safety plays
- A safety play sacrifices a possible overtrick to **guarantee the contract**
  against a bad break. Example: with a suit missing the queen and enough length,
  cash the ace first (or duck a round) to guard against a specific 4-0 or 3-0
  split, when an extra trick isn't needed.

## 9. Endplays and throw-ins
- **Throw-in:** deliberately give a defender the lead at a point where any return
  helps declarer (leads into a tenace, or concedes a ruff-and-discard).
- Strip the hand of safe exit cards first (eliminate side suits), then throw the
  defender in.

## 10. Squeezes (basics)
- A **squeeze** forces a defender to discard a winner because they cannot guard
  two suits at once. Requirements: declarer is (usually) down to needing all but
  one of the remaining tricks ("one loser, rectify the count"), threats in at
  least two suits, an entry to the threat that's established, and the guards held
  by the same defender.
- **Simple squeeze:** one defender guards two suits; cashing the "squeeze card"
  forces a fatal discard. Most squeezes need the count rectified (lose your
  losers early).

## 11. Defensive play technique
- **Second hand low, third hand high** (general guidelines, not absolutes).
- **Cover an honor with an honor** when it can promote a trick for your side.
- Lead and signal per `references/leads-and-signals.md` (attitude/count/suit-
  preference) to coordinate with partner.
- **Count declarer's hand**: use the bidding and the signals to reconstruct the
  unseen hands and find the setting trick.
- **Don't help declarer:** avoid leading new suits that finesse your own side,
  and avoid giving ruff-and-discards unless it can't cost.
