# Opening Leads and Defensive Signals (SAYC)

Bidding ends; the defense begins. SAYC pairs play standard leads and standard
("Standard American") attitude-first signals unless the card says otherwise.

## 1. Choosing which suit to lead
Priorities, roughly:
- **Partner's bid suit** — lead it unless you have a clear reason not to.
- **Your own long/strong suit** vs notrump (establish length tricks).
- **A sequence** (touching honors) is safe and attacking.
- **Trump lead** when declarer is marked with ruffing values you want to cut, or
  when nothing else is attractive.
- **Avoid** leading away from honors into declarer (e.g. away from an unsupported
  ace, or under an honor) unless you have a sequence; avoid leading dummy's long
  suit.
- vs **suit contracts**, prefer attacking leads (cash/establish before declarer
  draws trumps and pitches losers); singletons can set up ruffs.
- vs **notrump**, lead your long suit to set up length tricks.

## 2. Standard leads within a suit (which card)
From an honor sequence or holding, lead:

| Holding | Lead | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| AKx(x) | K (vs suit) | King from AK in standard leads |
| KQJ / KQ10 | K | top of sequence |
| QJ10 / QJ9 | Q | |
| J109 | J | |
| 1098 | 10 | |
| AKx vs **NT** | often **A then K**, or 4th-best from a long suit | depends on length |
| Three small (xxx) | top (high) — "top of nothing" | denies an honor |
| Two small (xx) | high (top of doubleton) | |
| Hxx (one honor) | **4th best** vs NT, low vs suit | "low from an honor" |
| Singleton | the card | sets up ruffs vs suit |

- **Fourth-best:** from a long suit headed by an honor against notrump, lead the
  **fourth-highest** card. Partner applies the **Rule of 11** (11 minus the led
  card's rank = number of higher cards in the other three hands).
- **Top of nothing** (high from xxx) tells partner you have no honor.

## 3. Leads vs notrump vs suit contracts
- vs **NT**: 4th-best from length; top of a sequence; you're racing to set up
  tricks, so attack your long suit.
- vs **suits**: top-of-sequence honor leads are safe; lead a singleton for a ruff;
  lead trumps to cut declarer's ruffs; don't lead away from tenaces (AQ, KJ) into
  declarer.

## 4. Attitude signals (standard — the SAYC default)
- When **following to partner's lead or signaling on declarer's lead**, a
  **high card = encourage** ("I like this suit / continue"), a **low card =
  discourage**. This is "standard attitude" (high-encourage). (UDCA — upside-down
  count and attitude — is the common variant where low encourages; flag it if the
  card specifies.)

## 5. Count signals
- When showing **count** (e.g. on declarer's led suit), play **high-low to show
  an even number** of cards, **low-then-high (up the line) to show an odd
  number**. This is standard count.
- Count helps partner know when declarer's long suit will run and when to hold up
  an ace.

## 6. Suit-preference signals (Lavinthal)
- In situations where attitude and count are already known/irrelevant (e.g.,
  giving partner a ruff, or an obvious singleton lead), a **high card asks for the
  higher-ranking of the two obvious remaining suits**, a **low card asks for the
  lower-ranking suit**.
- Classic use: leading a suit for partner to ruff, the size of the card you lead
  tells partner which suit to return.

## 7. Discards
- Standard discarding: a **high discard in a suit = "I like this suit"** (attitude
  discard), a low discard = "I don't care for it." (Again, UDCA reverses this.)
- Many pairs play **odd-even** or **Lavinthal discards** as variants; SAYC default
  is standard attitude discards. Discard from suits you don't need to protect.
