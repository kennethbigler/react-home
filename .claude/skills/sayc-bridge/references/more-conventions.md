## 1. Flannery 2♦ (variant — NOT pure SAYC; flag it)
- A 2♦ opening showing **exactly 5 hearts and 4 spades**, 11–15 HCP (a hand
  awkward to open 1♥ and rebid). Replaces the weak-2♦ in partnerships that adopt
  it. SAYC default keeps 2♦ as a weak two — only use Flannery if the card says so.
- **Responses:** 2♥/2♠ = to play (preference); 2NT = artificial inquiry asking
  opener to clarify shape/strength (opener shows shortness or a minor feature);
  3♣/3♦ = natural game tries; 4♥ = to play.

## 2. Gerber — full details and follow-ups
- **4♣ as Gerber** applies only as a direct jump over a natural **1NT or 2NT**
  (and over a 1NT/2NT rebid by partner). It is the ace-ask when notrump is the
  strain (since 4NT there is quantitative).
- **Ace responses:** 4♦ = 0 or 4 aces, 4♥ = 1, 4♠ = 2, 4NT = 3.
- **King ask:** after the ace reply, **5♣** asks for kings by the same steps:
  5♦ = 0/4, 5♥ = 1, 5♠ = 2, 5NT = 3 kings.
- **Signoff:** asker bids the final contract directly (e.g. 4NT/6NT) after the
  reply; bidding the agreed/again notrump is to play. If the ace reply already
  reveals two aces missing, sign off below slam.
- Do **not** use 4♣ as Gerber when a suit fit has been agreed — that's Blackwood
  territory; 4♣ becomes a cue bid/natural instead.

## 3. Lebensohl — the four main contexts (common add-on)
Lebensohl uses a **2NT relay to 3♣** ("slow") to distinguish weak vs strong hands
when the auction has been disrupted. Whole point: separate sign-off from a force.

- **(a) After partner's takeout double of a weak two** ((2♥) Dbl (P) to you):
  2NT = relay to 3♣, then pass/correct = weak; a direct 3-level suit = forcing/
  invitational. ("Slow shows" — confirm orientation with the card.)
- **(b) After our 1NT is overcalled** (1NT (2♥) ?): 2NT = relay to 3♣ then
  pass/correct = weak signoff; a direct new suit at the 3-level = game-forcing.
  Cue-bid of their suit = Stayman-ish; the **"fast/slow 3NT"** shows or denies a
  stopper in their suit.
- **(c) After opponents' weak two and our overcall** — similar weak/strong split.
- **(d) Over opponents' 1NT or after a reverse** — some pairs extend Lebensohl;
  partnership agreement required.

## 4. Drury — full follow-ups (common add-on)
- A passed hand bids **2♣** after partner's 3rd/4th-seat 1♥/1♠ opening to show a
  **limit raise (3+ trumps, ~10–12)**, guarding against a light third-seat opener.
- **Opener's rebid:** 2 of the major = "I have a sub-minimum/light opener, let's
  stop" (signoff); any other bid = a real opening, accept the invitation and head
  toward game. (Reverse Drury variant: opener's 2♦ shows the real opener.)
- **2-way Drury** (variant): 2♣ = 3-card limit raise, 2♦ = 4-card limit raise.

## 5. New Minor Forcing & Two-Way NMF (variant)
- **NMF (default):** after 1m–1M–1NT, responder bids the **other minor** as an
  artificial game try / checkback for 3-card support or a 4-card other major.
- **Two-Way NMF (variant):** 2♣ = any invitational hand (relay), 2♦ = any
  game-forcing checkback. Adds precision; flag it as non-default.
- Opener's replies to NMF: bid responder's major with 3, show a 4-card other
  major, bid 2NT/3NT by range, or rebid a 6-card minor.

## 6. Control-bid (cue-bid) sequences in detail
- After a trump fit + game force, a new suit below game = **first-round control**
  (ace or void), cheapest first, slam interest.
- Second round of cues shows **second-round controls** (king or singleton).
- Skipping a suit denies first-round control there — so partner can place the
  contract when a key control is missing (avoids slam off two cashing tricks in a
  suit).
- Cue-bidding is preferred to Blackwood when you have a void (Blackwood
  miscounts voids) or need to locate a *specific* suit control.

## 7. Grand Slam Force follow-ups
- **5NT (Josephine)** with no prior 4NT asks partner to bid 7 of the agreed
  trump with **2 of the top 3 honors**, else 6. Variants let partner bid an
  in-between step (6♣/6♦) to show 1 top honor plus extra length — partnership
  agreement.

## 8. Splinter follow-ups
- After a splinter (e.g. 1♥–4♣ = GF heart raise, club shortness), opener:
  - Signs off in 4 of the major with wasted values opposite the shortness
    (e.g. ♣Qxx facing the singleton).
  - Cue-bids / launches Blackwood with controls that work opposite the shortness.
- The splinterer has already limited the hand; opener becomes captain.

## 9. Maximal Overcall (game-try) Double
- In a competitive auction where the opponents' bid removes opener's natural
  game-try room (e.g. 1♠–(2♥)–2♠–(3♥) back to opener), a **double = a game try**
  in the agreed major (not penalty), since there's no bidding space left to try
  naturally. Common expert add-on.

## 10. Interference over Blackwood: DOPI / ROPI / DEPO
When an opponent interferes after your 4NT (RKC) ask:
- **DOPI** (over a cheap interference): **D**ouble = 0 (or 3) key cards, **P**ass
  = 1 (or 4), then cheapest steps for higher counts.
- **ROPI** (over a double): **R**edouble = 0/3, **P**ass = 1/4, steps for more.
- **DEPO** (over high interference, e.g. they jump): **D**ouble = **E**ven number
  of key cards, **P**ass = **O**dd number. Used when there's no room for steps.
